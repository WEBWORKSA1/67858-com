Lucky Numbers in Business: Why 8 Sells and 4 Is Avoided

By the 67858.com editorial teamUpdated October 27, 20267 min read

How cultural number symbolism shapes prices, phone numbers, addresses and domains — with practical rules for global businesses.

Numbers carry meaning far beyond arithmetic. In many East-Asian markets especially, the sound of a digit affects what people will pay for a phone number, which floor they want to live on and even which product price feels right. If you sell internationally, ignoring this is leaving money on the table.

The homophone principle

In Mandarin and Cantonese, several digits sound like other words. Those sound-alikes give digits their reputation:

DigitSounds likeReputation
8 (bā)fā — to prosperThe most prized digit for business
6 (liù)liú — to flow, smoothVery positive; “everything goes smoothly”
9 (jiǔ)jiǔ — long-lastingPositive; longevity
4 (sì)sǐ — deathWidely avoided
7 (qī)variesMixed: togetherness in some contexts, “gone” in others
5 (wǔ)wú — nothing / or “I” (wǒ) in combosNeutral to mixed

Real-world money effects

Premium numbers are traded like collectibles. Phone numbers made entirely of 8s have sold at auction for six-figure sums, and car plates rich in 8s and 6s attract bidding wars in several Asian markets. Buildings in some cities skip the fourth floor, or every floor ending in 4. The opening ceremony of the Beijing Olympics famously began at 8:08 pm on 8 August 2008.

Popular combinations

  • 168 — “prosperity all the way”, common in shop names and prices.
  • 518 — “I will prosper”.
  • 58 — read in some contexts as “I prosper”.
  • 520 — sounds like “I love you”; 20 May has become an informal romantic holiday.
  • 666 — “smooth”, and online slang for “impressive”.

Western number psychology

Western markets have their own quirks: prices ending in 9 or .99 (“charm pricing”) are perceived as cheaper than the next round number, while round prices can signal premium quality. Thirteen is avoided in some building and hotel floor numbering. Seven is broadly considered lucky.

Practical rules for global sellers

  1. Audit your prices by market. A price ending in 4 may underperform in some Chinese-speaking markets; one ending in 8 may outperform.
  2. Choose memorable contact numbers. Repeated digits and short patterns are easier to recall in any culture.
  3. Test, don't assume. Symbolism varies by region, generation and dialect. A/B test price endings before standardising.
