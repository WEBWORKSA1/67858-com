Quick answer: An EMI (equated monthly instalment) is the fixed payment that clears a loan — interest and principal — over its term. Early payments are mostly interest; later ones are mostly principal. The yearly chart shows that shift so you can see when extra payments save the most.
{% endraw %}{% include calc-howto.html title="Loan EMI Calculator" %}{% raw %}

Formula

EMI = P × i × (1 + i)^n ÷ ((1 + i)^n − 1) P = principal, i = annual rate ÷ 12, n = months

Worked example

Borrow 25,000 at 9% for 5 years: i = 0.0075 and n = 60, so the EMI is about 518.96. Over 60 payments you repay about 31,137.53, meaning roughly 6,137.53 is interest.

Frequently asked questions

Does a longer term lower the cost?

It lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest, because the balance stays outstanding longer.

Is this the same as APR?

This tool uses the nominal annual rate. APR may also include fees, which raises the true cost — add fees to the loan amount for a closer estimate.

Do prepayments help?

Yes. Extra principal early in the loan removes future interest on that amount for every remaining month.
